Therapist. Good afternoon, Maisie. Have you got a story from the last few days?
Child. I went to the optician.
Therapist. What happened at the appointment?
Child. They did the eye test and then they said I need glasses for the board.
Therapist. Who took you?
Child. My dad.
Therapist. Where did you go?
Child. The optician in town, opposite the Boots.
Therapist. What happened during the test?
Child. They showed me letters on the screen. Some were tiny. I had to say which way the E was facing.
Therapist. Was that easy or difficult?
Child. Easy at first. Then they made them smaller.
Therapist. What did you do when they got smaller?
Child. I guessed one. It was the wrong way.
Therapist. How did you feel when you got it wrong?
Child. I was embarrassed. I know it's a test, but I should have got it.
Therapist. What makes you say you should have got it?
Child. Because I can see the board in class, mostly. I just can't read the small writing from the back.
Therapist. What happened after the eye test?
Child. The optician tried some frames on me. I chose black ones, not the pink ones Dad picked.
Therapist. Did Dad agree with your choice?
Child. He said black looked better anyway.
Therapist. And what happened at the end of the appointment?
Child. They said the glasses would be ready next week. Dad took me to get a drink afterwards.
Therapist. You had the eye test, found the smallest letters difficult, chose black frames instead of the pink ones, and were told the glasses would be ready next week. Then you went for a drink with Dad.
Child. Yes.
Therapist. Is there anything else about the appointment you'd like me to know?
Child. No.
Therapist. Thanks, Maisie. That's us finished.
Child. Bye.
