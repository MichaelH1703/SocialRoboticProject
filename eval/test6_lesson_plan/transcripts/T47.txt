Therapist. Hi Priya. What would you like to tell me about?
Child. I went to a concert.
Therapist. Oh, brilliant. I've got a story too. I saw a band at a small venue once, and the queue for the cloakroom took longer than I expected. What concert did you go to?
Child. Olivia Rodrigo.
Therapist. Who did you go with?
Child. My older sister.
Therapist. Where was the concert?
Child. The O2 in London.
Therapist. What happened when you arrived?
Child. We had to queue for security. They checked our bags and scanned the tickets.
Therapist. What happened once you were inside?
Child. We bought water. Then we found our seats. We were quite high up.
Therapist. What was it like when the music started?
Child. The bass was so strong I could feel it in my ribs. Everyone was singing and the lights kept flashing.
Therapist. How did you feel?
Child. Happy. I knew nearly all the words.
Therapist. Did you sing along?
Child. Yes, but not for every song. I wanted to hear the quieter ones properly.
Therapist. Was there anything that you found difficult?
Child. The lights in the bit between songs. They kept going black and then white really fast. I had to shut my eyes for a minute.
Therapist. That sounds overwhelming.
Child. It wasn't all overwhelming. I liked most of it.
Therapist. You're right; I made it sound as if the whole concert was difficult. What happened near the end?
Child. She sang the song with the purple lights. My sister knew I wanted to hear it live.
Therapist. What happened when the concert finished?
Child. Everyone went out at once. We waited inside for a bit, then got the Tube.
Therapist. You went with your sister to see Olivia Rodrigo at the O2, queued for security, found your seats, enjoyed singing along and shut your eyes briefly when the lights changed quickly. After the show, you waited inside before taking the Tube.
Child. Yeah.
Therapist. Thanks, Priya. We'll stop there.
Child. Can we listen to music next time?
Therapist. We can talk about what would be useful next time.
