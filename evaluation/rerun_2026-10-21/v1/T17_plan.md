### 1. Narrative Summary

- The child described a situation where their tooth came out while they were eating an apple with grandad's help. They then showed the tooth to their mother who was excited by the event.

### 2. Communication Strengths

- **Clarity**: The child provided clear and complete information, describing events in chronological order.
- **Detailing**: The child detailed how they felt (surprised) and what happened without prompting, indicating a good grasp of story-telling basics.
- **Empathy & Sharing**: The child showed empathy by mentioning that the event didn't hurt.

### 3. Communication Gaps

- **Use of Future Tense**: There was no use of future tense to narrate potential outcomes (e.g., "We will talk about this later.")
- **Complex Vocabulary Use**: The child did not use complex vocabulary or sentences, which might need more encouragement.
- **Self-reflection & Empathy Development**: The child demonstrated empathy but could have further developed self-reflection skills.

### 4. Emotional Understanding

- The child clearly identified and expressed feelings such as surprise and excitement, showing emotional understanding of the situation.

### 5. Recommended Future Lesson Focus

Future sessions should focus on teaching the child to use more varied sentence structures and incorporate future tense in storytelling. Additionally, developing self-reflection skills might help the child understand their own emotions better and empathize with others' situations more deeply.

### 6. Suggested Reachy Prompts (simple, natural, child-friendly)

1. **Future Tense Practice**:
   - "Can we practice using 'I will' in a story? Like you said."
   
2. **Self-Reflection Prompt**: 
   - "Tell me what it was like to feel surprised today."

3. **Enhanced Empathy Development**:
   - "Do you remember how your grandad helped you? How did that make you feel?"

4. **Story Completion Activity**:
   - "Let's finish the story together. What happened after you showed the tooth to mommy?"
   
5. **Complex Vocabulary Introduction**:
   - "We can use big words like 'excited' and 'surprised'. Can we try using these in your stories?"

By focusing on these areas, both therapist-led sessions and future robot interactions will help the child improve their communication skills while also fostering emotional development.
